I found out that some LLM tools might demonstrate a bias when used to review a publication proposal. Meseems, they are a bit 'flattering', if this human trait can be used to characterize ChartGPT printout. However, I and ChatGPT, we have somewhat different ideas of what can please a reviewee (me in my case). What happened: I asked an expert of high esteem to have a look at my narrative explaining 'transcartesian' application ideas. The expert did more than I hoped to get: they struck up a 'conversation' with ChatGPT themed on 'transcartesian pixel enumeration'. Of course, I might do it for myself, but I have zero experience of using ChatGPT; what's more, I never reviewed a publication, even in pre-AI times; I just don't know a protocol. Being that ignorant, I, in particular, was afraid, that the model might happen to be trained with the texts I authored and saved on my computer, or it could use my digital footprint in any other enigmatic way. As I've never had mail exchanges with the expert before, I hoped this mysterious effect is ruled out for the ChatGPT conversation by the expert.

Gmail's Gemini composed an overview of ChatGPT's review sent to me with the expert's email:

≡+ AI Overview

  • Expert passed your ideas to ChatGPT for review; comments provided below detail novelty, relation to Hilbert/Z-order, and suggested publication framing.
  • Key distinction: Your structure changes discrete geometry first, unlike similar ordering methods imposing layout on Cartesian structure.
  • ChatGPT suggests focusing research question on reducing memory latency via non-Cartesian representation, distinct from existing methods.
  • Crucially, you must quantify memory locality using distance metrics or perform cache behavior benchmarks (L1/L2 misses, throughput) comparing your layout against raster, Morton, and Hilbert orders before serious publication.
  • Work is ready for GitHub/conceptual paper but needs quantitative data for a serious JPEG XS advantage paper.

In general, this is what I'd expected to see in the review, except that I'd never looked up Morton/Z-order and Hilbert ordering publications before. What distinguishes my OPINION from ChatGPT's, is that the investigation novelty is not the first priority for me; ChatGPT 'assumes' that I would rejoice over the fact that no one have ever tried to change discrete geometry in the first round, as existing ordering methods do their ordering by imposing layout on Cartesian structure. For me this 'novelty' means that before I'll be able to use transcartesian pixel enumeration in applications, I have to perform some hard toil, like examining cache behavior with hypothetical transcartesian-respecting memory organization and comparing it against raster, Morton, and Hilbert orders (is Intel caching solution applicable to memory organization respecting transcartesian pixel enumeration?). I would like to exchange the publication priority for a reference to transcartesian-respecting memory organization implementation, rather than receive a review emphazising that I have yet to develop one. I prefer the development resource over investigation priority. In a word, this ChatGPT bias reflects the body of documents used for training in this area of conversations. The documents, most probably, are scientific articles and journal expert reviews. And for an article to be accepted, it must feature 'novelty'.

It seems ChatGPT is trained to 'encourage' developers using flattering comments on their ideas.

Besides, LLM would be a great asset for technology innovators, if the LLM use were made mandatory for business and university research facility decision-makers, and the LLM reviews, when generated by decision-makers, were equally favorable. Maybe they are, and we only need to promote ChatGPT to the decision-making positions? ;)

To increase the chance of 'transcartesian' idea eligibility for desigion-makers, maybe it's worth to mention the possible application of transcartesian representation for string theory simulations (dimensionality compactification).
